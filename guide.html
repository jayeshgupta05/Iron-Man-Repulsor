#04 THE CORE PHYSICS & FORMULAS EXPLAINED SIMPLY

Four essential laser physics concepts broken down with intuitive real-world analogies.

1. THE RESONANCE CONDITION ($2nL = m\lambda$)

BOUNDARY MATCH

The Equation: $2nL = m\lambda \implies m = \frac{2nL}{\lambda}$

The Simple Analogy (The Guitar String): When you pluck a guitar string of length $L$, only specific musical notes (harmonics) can resonate because the string is pinned at both ends. Light trapped between two mirrors behaves identically: only waves whose half-wavelengths fit an exact integer number of times ($m=1, 2, 3\dots$) sustain constructive interference.

Viva Answer: "If $m$ is an exact integer, waves reinforce each other after every round trip. If $m$ has a fraction, waves cancel each other out destructively."

2. FREE SPECTRAL RANGE (FSR)

PEAK SPACING

The Equation: $\Delta\nu_{\text{FSR}} = \frac{c}{2nL} \quad \text{or} \quad \Delta\lambda_{\text{FSR}} \approx \frac{\lambda^2}{2nL}$

The Simple Analogy (Piano Keys): FSR is the distance between two consecutive resonant peaks on the graph (e.g. between $m=12$ and $m=13$).

How it behaves:
• Shorter Cavity ($L \downarrow$): Peaks spread far apart.
• Longer Cavity ($L \uparrow$): Peaks pack tightly together like closely spaced teeth on a comb.

3. REFLECTIVITY & CAVITY FINESSE ($\mathcal{F}$)

SHARPNESS / Q-FACTOR

The Equation: $F = \frac{4R}{(1-R)^2}, \quad \mathcal{F} = \frac{\pi\sqrt{R}}{1-R}$

The Simple Analogy (Filter Sieve):
• Low $R$ ($30\%$): Wide sieve holes. Peaks are broad, muddy hills.
• High $R$ ($95\%$): Ultra-fine mesh. Peaks become razor-sharp needle spikes with extreme wavelength purity.

Viva Answer: "Higher mirror reflectivity traps photons for more round trips, multiplying the selectivity and quality factor (Q-factor) of the cavity."

4. AIRY TRANSMISSION FUNCTION ($T$)

NET EFFICIENCY

The Equation: $T = \frac{1}{1 + F \sin^2(m\pi)}$

The Big Mystery Faculty Love to Ask: "If both mirrors reflect 95% of light, how can 100% of light pass through at resonance?"

The Mind-Blowing Answer: Because inside the cavity, light builds up to over $20\times$ its incoming brightness through constructive interference! Even though only $5\%$ leaks out through $M_2$, $5\% \times 20 = 100\%$. Destructive cancellation in the reverse direction eliminates back-reflection completely!

#05 EINSTEIN'S LASER RATE EQUATIONS & GAIN MEDIUM

Why Tony Stark's gauntlet needs an active medium, and how light amplification actually happens.

1. SPONTANEOUS EMISSION
⏳ ⚛ ➔ ✨
Rate = A₂₁ · N₂

Excited atom randomly decays on its own, spitting out an uncontrolled photon in a random direction (incoherent light like a lightbulb).

2. STIMULATED ABSORPTION
✨ + ⚛(E₁) ➔ ⚛*(E₂)
Rate = B₁₂ · ρ · N₁

A ground-state atom eats a passing photon and jumps to the excited state. This causes optical loss/attenuation.

3. STIMULATED EMISSION (LASER!)
✨ + ⚛*(E₂) ➔ ✨✨ + ⚛
Rate = B₂₁ · ρ · N₂

A passing photon tickles an excited atom, forcing it to drop and emit a twin clone photon with exact same phase, color, and direction!

THE COMPLETE EINSTEIN DIFFERENTIAL EQUATION IN OUR CODE: SOLVED PER FRAME
$$\frac{dN_2}{dt} = \underbrace{W \cdot N_1}_{\text{Pumping (Arc Reactor)}} - \underbrace{B_{21}\rho \cdot (N_2 - N_1)}_{\text{Stimulated Transitions}} - \underbrace{A_{21} \cdot N_2}_{\text{Spontaneous Decay}}$$

Population Inversion Condition: Notice the term $(N_2 - N_1)$. Under normal room temperature, $N_1 \gg N_2$ (most atoms are ground), so absorption wins and laser beams cannot form. But when the Arc Reactor pump power $W$ pushes $N_2 > N_1$, net stimulated emission becomes positive, triggering the photon avalanche that powers the repulsor blast!

Q1 Why does transmission reach 100% at resonance even if mirrors reflect 95% of light? ▼

Quick 1-Sentence Answer:

Constructive interference causes light to build up inside the cavity to over $20\times$ incident brightness, so the small fraction leaking through equals 100% of the input beam!

Detailed Viva Answer:

At resonance, round-trip phase shift is an exact integer multiple of $2\pi$. The infinite series of internal bounces adds constructively in the forward direction and cancels out destructively in the reverse direction, meaning zero light is reflected backward and 100% passes forward.

Q2 What is the physical meaning of the Mode Number $m$? ▼

Quick 1-Sentence Answer:

$m$ is the exact number of half-wavelengths ($\lambda / 2$) of light standing between the two cavity mirrors.

Detailed Viva Answer:

From the boundary condition $L = m \cdot (\lambda / 2n)$, $m$ also directly equals the number of stationary electric field nodes ($E=0$) formed along the cavity axis, as visualized by the white dots on our Field Overlay canvas.

Q3 What happens if you make both mirrors 100% reflective ($R_1 = R_2 = 1.0$)? ▼

Quick 1-Sentence Answer:

The cavity would store light forever without loss, but you could NEVER emit a laser beam because zero light could escape!

Detailed Viva Answer:

A practical laser requires an Output Coupler with reflectivity slightly below 1.0 (typically $R_2 \approx 85\% - 95\%$) so that a controlled fraction of coherent photons can exit as the useful repulsor beam.

Q4 What does Free Spectral Range (FSR) represent and how does cavity length affect it? ▼

Quick 1-Sentence Answer:

FSR is the frequency or wavelength spacing between two consecutive resonance peaks ($\Delta\lambda = \lambda^2 / 2nL$).

Detailed Viva Answer:

Longer cavities have smaller FSR (peaks pack closer together). Shorter cavities have larger FSR (peaks spread far apart). In micro-cavities ($L \sim 1\,\mu\text{m}$), FSR is so wide that only a single longitudinal mode can exist.

Q5 How did you fix the pointer glitching in disassembled mode? ▼

Quick 1-Sentence Answer:

We implemented sticky hand arbitration with elevation hysteresis ($\Delta Y > 0.14$) and a 400ms debounce window to stop camera noise from flipping between left and right hands.

Detailed Viva Answer:

Raw MediaPipe coordinates fluctuate by $\pm 0.005$ due to camera sensor noise. Per-frame height comparison was flipping hand assignments 60 times a second, dragging the cursor across the screen. Hysteresis locks to the active hand, while card-aware stabilization keeps large stage cards from magnetically jerking the reticle.

Q6 Why can't you build a 2-level steady-state laser? Why do real lasers need 3 or 4 levels? ▼

Quick 1-Sentence Answer:

In a 2-level system, stimulated emission equals absorption ($B_{21} = B_{12}$), so pumping can at best reach 50/50 transparency, never inversion.

Detailed Viva Answer:

Once $N_2 = N_1$, the rate of atoms pumped up equals the rate stimulated back down. A 3-level or 4-level laser (like Nd:YAG) solves this by pumping to an upper level that decays ultra-rapidly into a metastable state with a long lifetime, enabling massive population buildup ($N_2 \gg N_1$).
